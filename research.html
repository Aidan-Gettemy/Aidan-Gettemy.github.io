Research

My work asks how to learn from physical models and sensor data when the unknowns are high-dimensional and the simulations are expensive. Below are three projects, with my collaborators, our goals, and my role.

Bayesian inverse problems

With Felipe Pereira (UT Dallas), Marcio Borges (LNCC), and R. Guiraldello · Paper in preparation

The problem. Subsurface properties such as permeability cannot be measured everywhere, so we infer them from limited pressure data. A Bayesian approach returns a whole distribution of plausible fields rather than one guess, but sampling that distribution with standard MCMC becomes very slow when the field has very many unknowns.

Our goal. To build a global-local multiscale MCMC method for domain-decomposed Darcy flow models that explores the posterior faster, while keeping the sampler faithful to the prior on the underlying Gaussian random field.

What I contributed. I developed and implemented the sampling framework, including prior-preserving proposal mechanisms for fields parameterized by Karhunen–Loève expansions. I ran the large-scale experiments on a university HPC cluster (SLURM, MPI) and evaluated convergence with R-hat, coverage, reconstruction error, and the posterior fields themselves. I have presented this work at SIAM UQ26, the SIAM Texas–Louisiana Section meeting, and other venues.

Surrogate modeling

With Susan Minkoff, John Zweck, and Elaine Spiller · Wind Energy Science, 2026

The problem. Erosion on the leading edge of a wind turbine blade degrades performance, and catching it early matters. Physics-based simulations can show how erosion changes a turbine's behavior, but they are too slow to run repeatedly in a monitoring setting.

Our goal. To use machine learning surrogate models, trained on simulations, to classify the severity of leading-edge erosion, as a step toward digital-twin condition monitoring.

What I contributed. I simulated turbine behavior under varying inflow conditions and erosion severity with OpenFAST, and wrote a MATLAB framework to automate the simulations and the processing of their output. I then evaluated supervised learning and Gaussian process emulator models for detecting erosion, and contributed the analysis, figures, and manuscript text.

  • GitHub: surrogate modeling code

NTF modeling

With L. Abbatessa and Nathan Post · Roux Institute and WindESCo · Under review

The problem. Wake steering deliberately yaws an upstream turbine to improve the output of the turbines behind it, but what a turbine actually does can differ from what was planned. Operational SCADA data records what happened, and it is noisy and incomplete.

Our goal. To evaluate yawed turbine transfer functions from SCADA data, and to understand the biases that creep in when comparing steered and unsteered turbines.

What I contributed. I built Python data pipelines for utility-scale SCADA data, along with filtering and visualization tools. I used regression-based analysis to identify yaw misalignment biases, and developed consensus, random forest, and Gaussian process models to estimate unobserved turbine states and reconstruct spatial wind fields. I presented the results at NAWEA/WindTech 2025.
